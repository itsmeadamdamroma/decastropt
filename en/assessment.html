What the assessment measures

The initial evaluation captures the objective parameters that set your starting point. No guesswork: only data to build your programming on.

BMI (Body Mass Index)

The Body Mass Index relates weight to height and places you in a reference band (underweight, normal, overweight, obesity). It isn't a perfect metric because it can't separate lean mass from fat, but it gives a useful first picture to contextualize your goal and compare it against the measurements that follow.

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)

Basal metabolic rate is the number of calories your body burns at rest to keep vital functions running: breathing, circulation, temperature regulation. Knowing your BMR means knowing the floor below which you shouldn't drop without metabolic risk. It's the zero point every nutrition calculation starts from.

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure)

TDEE is your total daily energy expenditure: BMR plus the energy spent on work, training and NEAT (non-exercise activity thermogenesis). The TDEE value sets your maintenance calories, from which we calculate the deficit or surplus your goal needs.

Body composition

Body composition goes beyond total weight and separates lean mass, fat mass and water. It's estimated with skinfold calipers using the Jackson-Pollock 3- or 7-site protocol, which measures skinfold thickness at specific points. The result lets us set fat and muscle targets that match reality, not just the number on the scale.

Posture & movement screening

A posture assessment and movement-pattern screen identify compensations, asymmetries and mobility limits before loading the body with weight. Preventing an injury costs a minute of observation; rehabbing one costs months. That's why the assessment includes joint mobility tests and static posture observation.

Why the assessment is the first step

Skipping the evaluation means training blind. Data turn a generic program into one that's about you.

Data-driven programming vs. a standard template

A program found on the internet is written for an average person who doesn't exist. Two people with the same goal can have TDEEs that differ by 500 kcal, opposite mobility and completely different recovery. Only starting from your own numbers can you get volume, intensity and exercises calibrated to you. Read more in our article on calculating BMI, BMR and TDEE.

Injury prevention

The assessment catches risk factors before they become problems: mobility limits, left-right asymmetries, latent pain, medications that affect heart rate or temperature regulation. Knowing in advance that you have chronic lower-back pain or an unstable shoulder changes exercise selection, ranges of motion and load progression. See the full picture in our article on body assessment.

Baseline & progress tracking

The first assessment sets your starting line: your numbers, your posture, your body composition at time zero. Without a baseline you can't measure progress objectively — you're left with a feeling. Periodic measurements show whether the program is working, when to recalibrate, and make results visible that would otherwise stay invisible.

Tools & methods

Standardized, scientifically validated protocols. No invented metrics or improvised tools.

Jackson-Pollock skinfold protocol

Body-composition estimation follows the Jackson-Pollock protocol, the most widely used and studied in the sports literature. The 3-site version is quick and suited to non-clinical settings; the 7-site version is more precise. Both return a body-fat percentage to compare against reference tables by age and sex.

Basal-metabolism equations

BMR is calculated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, updated and validated on modern populations. For individuals with high muscle mass we also consider the Katch-McArdle equation, which incorporates lean mass into the calculation. Choosing the best equation depends on the individual's profile.

TDEE multipliers

TDEE is BMR multiplied by an activity factor, chosen based on training level, daily work and NEAT. The multipliers range from 1.2 (sedentary) to 1.9+ (athlete with intense training and physical work). Selecting the right factor is where a rough estimate introduces the most error — and where a personalized assessment makes the difference.

Movement screening & mobility tests

The movement tests assess the quality of the fundamental motor patterns: squat, hinge, push, pull, lunge and rotation. Watching how the body moves unloaded reveals mobility deficits, instability and compensations. This screening determines which exercises to include right away and which to prepare before loading.
