The method

Data first

I always start from real data: weight, height, age, activity level, injury history. From those numbers I calculate BMI, BFR, BMR and TDEE. From there I build volume, intensity and recovery. No generic templates, no copied protocols.

A technical PDF

Every program is a technical PDF: exercises, sets, reps, recovery, weekly progression. You can take it to the gym and follow it without doubts.

Follow-up

I don't leave you alone with a PDF. Monthly follow-up recalibrates the program on your real progress.

My training philosophy

Boxing taught me that pain is a data point, not an enemy. In the ring you can't fake it: either you're prepared or you get knocked out. I bring that mindset to every program I write. I don't promise 30-day transformations because I know the body doesn't work that way. I promise method, data, progression.

The marathon taught me long-term management. A 16-week plan for 42km isn't "run more": it's dosing volume, intensity and recovery. Same with programs: it's not doing more, it's doing the right thing at the right time.

The Sports Science degree gave me the scientific base. Anatomy, physiology, biomechanics, pathology. When I build a program, I know why an exercise is there, why that number of reps, why that recovery. It's not intuition: it's applied physiology. If an exercise doesn't work on my body, I don't put it in yours.
